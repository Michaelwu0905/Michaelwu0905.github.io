The line graph illustrates the percentage of individuals in four distinct age groups in the UK who went to the cinema at least once a week between 2001 and 2010.

Overall, cinema attendance experienced an upward trend across all age groups over the ten-year period. While the youngest age group continuously had the highest attendance rates, the oldest group showed the most dramatic growth. By the end of the period, the gap of percentage of different age groups had significantly narrowed.

In 2001, the 15-24 age group had the highest participation rate at approximately 42%. Although this figure fluctuated significantly, experiencing a sharp decline to 31% in 2002 before rising back, it remained at the top for most of the decade. By 2010, after several smaller fluctuations, it dropped to around 32%.

The other three groups all saw a substantial rise in attendance. The 25-34 group showed the steadiest growth, climbing consistently from around 22% in 2001 to overtake the 15-24 group in 2010, at 37%. Similarly, the 35-44 group saw its participation rate nearly doubled, from 16% to a peak of almost 30% in 2007, before dropped to 28% in 2010. The most dramatic increase happened in the 45+ age group, which began with the lowest participation rate of about 13%. Its figure grew slowly at first but had a significant rise after 2006, reaching over 30% by 2010, ending with a higher percentage than the 35-44 group.
