Here is a sample IELTS essay responding to the prompt: **“To what extent do you agree with the viewpoint that science should be aimed to improve people’s lives?”**

---

Science has long been a driving force behind human progress, from curing diseases to enabling global communication. While some argue that science should pursue knowledge for its own sake, I strongly agree that its primary aim should be to improve people’s lives, though with important qualifications.

On one hand, science directed at tangible human benefits has delivered undeniable gains. Medical research, for instance, has eradicated smallpox and developed vaccines that save millions of lives annually. Agricultural science has boosted crop yields, alleviating hunger in developing nations. Environmental science has guided policies to reduce pollution and combat climate change. These examples show that when science prioritises human wellbeing, it not only solves pressing problems but also builds public trust and justifies continued investment in research.

However, it is important to recognise that not all scientific inquiry yields immediate practical applications. Foundational discoveries—such as the structure of DNA or the laws of electromagnetism—were driven by curiosity rather than direct utility. Yet these breakthroughs later became the basis for life-saving medicines and global electricity grids. Thus, a rigid focus on short-term benefits could stifle the very discoveries that ultimately generate the greatest improvements to human life. Science needs room for basic research, even when its applications are not immediately obvious.

Nevertheless, I believe that even pure research must ultimately be justified by its potential to serve humanity. Science is a human endeavour, funded by society and conducted within ethical limits. Therefore, research that has no foreseeable benefit, or that causes harm—such as developing more lethal weapons—cannot be supported simply in the name of knowledge. The goal of improving lives also imposes moral boundaries: scientific progress should not come at the cost of human dignity, privacy, or environmental sustainability.

In conclusion, while I strongly agree that science should ultimately aim to improve people’s lives, I do not advocate for a narrow, utilitarian approach that dismisses basic research. Instead, a balanced vision is needed—one that values curiosity-driven discovery while always keeping human wellbeing as the final benchmark of scientific success.
